An Agnostic Reads: Mark 9-16

July 2026

Mark 9-16

The pattern from the first half continues. Jesus predicts his death three times, and each time his disciples respond by talking about power. Who gets to sit next to him. Who is the greatest. They still want a king.

But others start to see. Bartimaeus, a blind beggar, calls out "Son of David" while the disciples try to shut him up. A scribe asks an honest question, and Jesus tells him he's close to the kingdom. The people you'd expect to understand don't. The ones you'd write off do.

Gethsemane is hard to read. Jesus asks God to take this cup from him. He prays three times. The disciples fall asleep three times. We talked about this: does Jesus have his own will here? It seems like he does. And he gives it up. "Not what I want, but what you want."

After that, everything moves. Judas kisses him. Peter lies three times. Everyone runs. There's a young man who flees naked — only Mark mentions this. No one stays.

The crucifixion is sparse. Mark doesn't give us Pilate washing his hands or a spear in Jesus' side. Just darkness and one sentence: "My God, why have you forsaken me?"
We didn't agree on what that means. Real abandonment? A quote from Psalm 22 that ends in hope? Maybe it's both. Mark doesn't tell us.

Then the centurion. A Roman soldier, watching Jesus’ die, says: "This man was the Son of God." Not Peter. Not John. A pagan executioner at the cross.

The women find an empty tomb. A man in white says Jesus has risen. They run away and tell no one because they're afraid.

And that's it, that's how Mark ends. The verses after that were added later. The original Gospel ends in silence. The disciples never got it. The women fled. And here we are, still asking the same question Mark started with.
